# Set Builder — Proposed Work List

_Agreed 21 November 2026 — all works (ticked and unticked) are now in the Set Builder library. Exception: Dvořák String Quartet No. 11 has no Apple recording available in Japan, so it is left out for now._

**How to review:** `[x]` = proposed, `[ ]` = optional extra (off unless you tick it). Strike out, untick or add anything — then tell me (or edit this file) and I'll research the final list.

Years are the year each work was **completed** (the game's rule). They are my working dates; every one gets double-checked during research. ⚠︎ marks a date that sources disagree on — I note what I'd use.

---

## Decisions needed first

### A. Same-year works
Several composers wrote multiple works of one type in the same year (e.g. all three Schumann quartets are 1842). Two ways to handle it:

1. **Keep "every work in a set has a different year"** (current rule). Some combinations can't fill a set alone, so the builder will say "pick another composer or ensemble too". _← my recommendation_
2. **Allow same-year works** in a set; either order counts as correct. More combinations work, but those pairs become free points.

### B. Ensemble names
Proposed builder options: **String quartets** · **Solo piano** · **Orchestral**.
"Solo piano" instead of "Piano sonatas" because several composers' best-loved piano music isn't sonatas (and Dvořák wrote none).

---

## How full is each combination?

Distinct years available with the proposed (ticked) list. A combination needs **6** distinct years for a 6-work set on its own, **10** for a 10-work set. Mixing composers/ensembles in the builder fixes any gap.

| | String quartets | Solo piano | Orchestral |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Beethoven** | 6 ✅ | 12 ✅ | 10 ✅ |
| **Schubert** | 4 | 6 ✅ | 6 ✅ |
| **Mendelssohn** | 5 (6 with Octet) | 5 | 6 ✅ |
| **Schumann** | 1 (all 1842) | 7 ✅ | 6 ✅ |
| **Brahms** | 2 (8 with the chamber extras) | 8 ✅ | 10 ✅ |
| **Dvořák** | 5 | 0 (1 with Humoresque) | 2 (6 with extras) |

**10-work sets** will almost always mix composers or ensembles — that's fine, and it's where the historical moments shine.

## Historical moments

Each has a one-line "what happened" shown after the reveal. The builder only uses a moment whose year isn't already taken by a work in the set, and that falls within (or close to) the set's time span. Up to **1** per 6-work set, **2** per 10-work set.

### Japan
- [x] **1823** — Philipp Franz von Siebold arrives at Dejima. _A German doctor at the Dutch trading post becomes a key channel for Western science into Japan._
- [x] **1831** — Hokusai's _The Great Wave off Kanagawa_ is published. _Part of his series Thirty-six Views of Mount Fuji, it becomes the world's most famous Japanese print._ ⚠︎ (c. 1830–32)
- [x] **1837** — Ōshio Heihachirō's uprising in Osaka. _A former official leads a revolt against officials' indifference during the Tenpō famine._
- [x] **1860** — The Sakuradamon Incident. _Chief minister Ii Naosuke is assassinated outside Edo Castle in the snow._
- [x] **1868** — The Meiji Restoration; Edo is renamed Tokyo. _Imperial rule is restored and the shogunate ends after more than 250 years._
- [x] **1871** — The Iwakura Mission sets out. _Japanese leaders begin a nearly two-year tour of the US and Europe to study the West._
- [x] **1872** — Japan's first railway opens, Shimbashi–Yokohama. _A 29 km line, built with British help._
- [x] **1873** — Japan adopts the Western (Gregorian) calendar. _The lunar calendar is dropped; 3 December 1872 becomes 1 January 1873._
- [x] **1877** — The Satsuma Rebellion. _Saigō Takamori's samurai revolt is defeated by the new conscript army — the end of the samurai era._
- [x] **1883** — The Rokumeikan opens in Tokyo. _A Western-style hall where Japan's elite hold balls and concerts to impress foreign diplomats._
- [x] **1889** — The Meiji Constitution is proclaimed. _Japan becomes the first constitutional monarchy in Asia._
- [x] **1894** — The First Sino-Japanese War begins. _Its outcome signals Japan's arrival as a modern military power._

### Europe
- [x] **1804** — Napoleon crowns himself Emperor of the French. _Beethoven famously scratched his name from the dedication of the Eroica in response._
- [x] **1805** — The Battle of Trafalgar. _Nelson's fleet defeats the French and Spanish navies; Nelson is killed._
- [x] **1812** — Napoleon's retreat from Moscow. _His Grande Armée of over 400,000 men is destroyed by the Russian winter._
- [x] **1815** — The Battle of Waterloo. _Napoleon's final defeat ends more than two decades of war in Europe._
- [x] **1825** — The Stockton & Darlington Railway opens. _The world's first public railway to use steam locomotives._
- [x] **1837** — Queen Victoria comes to the throne. _She reigns for 63 years._ _(same year as Ōshio — builder picks one)_
- [x] **1848** — Revolutions sweep across Europe. _Uprisings in Paris, Vienna, Berlin and beyond; Metternich flees Vienna._
- [x] **1851** — The Great Exhibition at the Crystal Palace, London. _A giant glass hall showcases industry from around the world._
- [x] **1859** — Darwin publishes _On the Origin of Species_. _His theory of evolution by natural selection reshapes science._
- [x] **1863** — The London Underground opens. _The world's first underground railway, with steam trains._
- [x] **1869** — The Suez Canal opens. _Ships can now sail between Europe and Asia without going around Africa._
- [x] **1871** — Germany is unified under Kaiser Wilhelm I. _The empire is proclaimed in the Hall of Mirrors at Versailles._ _(same year as Iwakura)_
- [x] **1889** — The Eiffel Tower opens in Paris. _Built for the World's Fair, it is the tallest structure in the world._ _(same year as the Meiji Constitution)_
- [x] **1895** — The Lumière brothers' first public film screening. _Paying audiences in Paris watch moving pictures for the first time._
